# Recording Session Script

**The questions below are prompts, not scripts.**

An interviewer reads the question. **The question is not recorded.** The volunteer answers in
their own words, and *that answer is the recording*. Then you write down what they actually
said, word for word, as the `reference`.

> **Do not write out an answer and read it aloud.** Read speech and spontaneous speech are
> measurably different — pacing, prosody, disfluencies — and the whole reason for recording
> real humans is to avoid choosing a provider that is good at clean, artificial speech. A
> scripted corpus would put us back where synthetic audio would have.
>
> If an answer comes out with "umm", a pause, or a correction, **that is a good take.** Keep it.

Each conversation is one continuous exchange. Ask question 1, get the answer, ask question 2,
and so on — the answers should sound like they follow each other.

**Target 5–15 seconds per answer. Hard ceiling 30 seconds.**

# The difficult behaviours — assign them to these slots

These are *how* something is said. Cover all thirteen; the slots below are suggestions that
fit the question naturally.

| Behaviour | Put it in | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| Digit-by-digit phone | `conv-02-t1`, `conv-08-t3` | Ask them to read it out slowly, one digit at a time |
| **Self-correction** | `conv-03-t2` | Ask for the expected range; a genuine "fifteen… sorry, eighteen" often just happens. If not, ask them to correct themselves mid-answer |
| **1–2 second pause mid-answer** | `conv-03-t3` | Ask a question that needs thought — "by what date do you need to decide?" Keep recording through the silence |
| **Long answer near 30 s** | `conv-01-t3` | "Walk me through your experience" — let them talk |
| **Very short answer** | `conv-04-t3` | Just "Notice period?" — "Ninety days." or "Haan." |
| Fast answer | `conv-04-t4` | Ask rapid-fire |
| Hesitant answer | `conv-04-t1` | Relocation is a question people hedge on naturally |
| Filler words | anywhere in `conv-04`, `conv-10` | Do not coach them out of it |
| **Nine/nineteen confusable** | `conv-01-t2` or `conv-03-t1` | An answer containing nineteen, ninety, fifteen or fifty |
| Mid-sentence code-switching | all of `conv-08`…`conv-11` | Ask in Hinglish |
| English technical terms in Hindi | `conv-06-t3`, `conv-07-t1` | CTC, notice period, join, relocate — these come naturally |
| Regional accent variation | second speaker's conversations | Whoever is not you |
| Background speech | the noisy set | Record where other people are talking |

# Noisy set — 5 conversations, 25 recordings

**Same words, different room.** Re-record these exact conversations somewhere genuinely
noisy — a street, a café, a room with a TV on.

| Record as | Re-record of | Language |
|---|---|---|
| `noisy-01` | `conv-01` | Indian English |
| `noisy-02` | `conv-05` | Hindi |
| `noisy-03` | `conv-08` | Hinglish |
| `noisy-04` | `conv-09` | Hinglish |
| `noisy-05` | `conv-02` | Indian English — **spare margin**, record if you can |

Play back or read out what was said in the quiet take, and have the volunteer say **the same
words** in the noisy room. Then copy that quiet turn's `reference`, `referenceAlt` and
`entities` across unchanged.

If the words come out different, it is a different utterance — and the comparison would then
be measuring the script rather than the noise.

# Practical notes for the session

- **Record each conversation as one file per turn**, named exactly as the worksheet says:
  `conv-01-t1.wav` … `noisy-05-t5.wav`.
- **Do not use real personal data.** Phone numbers must be invented digit strings that are
  not in service. Employer names should be made up.
- **Retake by overwriting.** Only the take you keep should exist in `audio/`.
- **Write the reference immediately after each conversation**, while you still remember what
  was actually said. Reconstructing it an hour later is how references drift from the audio.
- Budget a full day for 80 takes, two environments, and the references.
