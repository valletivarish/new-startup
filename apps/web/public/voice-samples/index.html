Same voice, every language — clarity tested

We had all 30 Google Chirp 3 HD voices read two short calls, a screening call and a support call full of easy-to-mishear details (a spelled code, a phone number, a date, a time, an amount and a name), in 52 languages. We then let Google's speech recogniser write down what it heard, once from the clean audio and once after squeezing the audio through an 8 kHz phone line like our Exotel calls, and compared it with what it heard from the other 29 voices, counting the characters that differ. All scores are error rates, so lower is clearer. The table ranks voices on the 11 languages we launch with in India: English, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Urdu, Kannada, Malayalam and Punjabi, the mother tongue of about 89% of Indians (Census 2011). The clarity score is the average error across those languages and both audio qualities, plus a penalty for the one language where the voice did worst compared with the other voices; the phone-line score is the error on the 8 kHz copy alone. Most voices came out very close, so the scores mainly catch voices that slip on numbers or names somewhere. Below are the 3 clearest female and 3 clearest male voices for India.
